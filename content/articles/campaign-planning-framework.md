A good campaign plan is not a fifty-slide strategy deck. It is a short operating document that makes the important decisions **before money and time start moving**.

## 1. Define the business outcome

Start with one primary result: revenue, qualified leads, booked calls, trials, registrations, or another measurable business action.

Traffic, impressions, and clicks may support the goal, but they should not replace it.

## 2. Describe the audience clearly

Write a useful description of the people you want to reach. Focus on the problem, intent, context, and buying situation—not a fictional persona filled with trivia.

Ask:

- What problem are they trying to solve?
- What would make them act now?
- What would stop them from converting?
- Where can you realistically reach them?

## 3. Make the offer specific

The campaign needs a clear reason to click and a clear next step after the click. Message, landing page, and offer should feel like one continuous experience.

## 4. Pick a primary channel

Choose the channel that best matches the job.

- Search captures existing demand.
- Paid social can create demand and test creative angles.
- Email works well for audiences you already have.
- SEO compounds over time when the topic has durable search demand.

You can add channels later. A focused first version is easier to measure.

## 5. Set the budget and timeframe

Decide how much you can spend and how long the campaign needs to run before you can reasonably judge it. Use the [Marketing Budget Calculator](/tools/marketing-budget) if you need a starting point.

## 6. Build tracking before launch

Decide what counts as a conversion, confirm the conversion can be measured, and use consistent campaign URLs. The [UTM Builder](/tools/utm-builder) makes the URL portion simple.

## 7. Write the decision rules

Before launch, decide what would make you:

- continue the campaign,
- increase budget,
- change the message,
- change the landing page,
- or stop the campaign.

This prevents every performance review from becoming an emotional argument.

## The one-page version

Your campaign plan can fit on one page:

1. Goal
2. Audience
3. Offer
4. Primary channel
5. Budget + duration
6. KPIs + tracking
7. Review date + decision rules
