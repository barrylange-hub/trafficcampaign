Campaign optimization is not constant tweaking. It is a disciplined loop: **find the biggest constraint, make a meaningful change, measure the result, then decide what to do next**.

## Step 1: Verify the measurement

Do not optimize against broken data. Confirm spend, traffic, conversions, and revenue or conversion value are being recorded well enough to support decisions.

## Step 2: Find the bottleneck

Work from the business result backward.

A paid campaign might look like this:

**Revenue → conversions → landing-page conversion rate → clicks → CTR → impressions**

Find the stage where performance is most obviously constrained.

## Step 3: Separate traffic problems from conversion problems

If CTR is weak, the problem may be targeting, creative, message, or intent.

If CTR is healthy but conversion rate is weak, look at message match, landing-page clarity, friction, offer strength, speed, or traffic quality.

The [CTR Calculator](/tools/ctr) and [Conversion Rate Calculator](/tools/conversion-rate) make those comparisons easy.

## Step 4: Check the economics

Better conversion metrics do not automatically mean a profitable campaign. Watch acquisition cost, revenue, margin, and return.

Use [ROAS](/tools/roas), [CAC](/tools/cac), and [Break-even ROAS](/tools/break-even-roas) together when evaluating paid acquisition.

## Step 5: Make one meaningful change

Avoid changing audience, creative, offer, landing page, and bidding strategy all at once unless the campaign is fundamentally broken.

A focused change creates cleaner evidence.

## Step 6: Give the change enough time

Do not react to every hour of data. The right review window depends on traffic volume, conversion frequency, sales cycle, and campaign economics.

## Step 7: Record what you learned

The value of testing is cumulative. Write down:

- what you changed,
- why you changed it,
- when it changed,
- what happened,
- and what you will do next.

Over time, this becomes a campaign-specific knowledge base instead of a series of repeated guesses.

## The optimization rule

Optimize the **constraint**, not the most visible metric.

A higher CTR is irrelevant if the extra clicks never become customers. A lower CPC is not useful if traffic quality collapses. Keep the business outcome at the top of the scorecard.
